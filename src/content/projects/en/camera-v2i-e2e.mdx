## Research question

**How much earlier can infrastructure-camera information enable pedestrian detection and deceleration when parked vehicles block the ego camera?**

The proposed system uses a **1/5-scale test vehicle**. A roadside camera observes the occluded area and sends pedestrian state to the vehicle, where an E2E planner combines it with the ego-camera input. The current work defines the requirements and comparison structure; implementation, training, and vehicle trials are planned.

## Occlusion scenario and operating domain

A stopped or parked vehicle hides a pedestrian from the ego camera near a school or on campus. The infrastructure camera is proposed on the opposite side of the occluded area, where it can observe the pedestrian earlier.

| Operating condition | Initial scope |
| --- | --- |
| Location | A controllable road near a school or on campus |
| Lighting | Daytime with sufficient visibility |
| Weather and surface | Clear weather and dry pavement |
| Vehicle motion | Low-speed driving on a straight section |
| Outside the initial scope | Night, adverse weather, intersections, and mixed traffic |

The **operational design domain (ODD)** specifies the conditions in which the system is intended to operate.

The current message scope is **`x`, `y`, `confidence`, and `timestamp`**. The edge device sends these object-state fields directly to the vehicle. The exact message format, coordinate conventions, synchronization, and communication protocol remain to be finalized.

## Pedestrian position from homography

The proposed geometry pipeline detects a pedestrian bounding box, selects its **bottom-center image point `(u, v)`** as an estimate of ground contact, and projects that point into road coordinates `(x, y)` using a calibrated homography:

<p class="design-equation"><code>s [x, y, 1]ᵀ = H [u, v, 1]ᵀ</code></p>

Calibration pairs image reference points with road-plane reference points to estimate `H`. The approach assumes that the pedestrian's ground-contact point lies on the calibrated road plane. Calibration and position-error measurements are planned.

## Minimum detection-distance requirement

The preliminary design links available response distance to system latency, braking distance, and a safety margin:

<p class="design-equation"><code>d_req = v · T_system + D_brake(v) + M</code></p>

| Term | Meaning in the design budget |
| --- | --- |
| `v · T_system` | Distance traveled during the end-to-end system delay |
| `D_brake(v)` | Braking distance at the selected speed, to be measured on the test vehicle |
| `M` | Required safety clearance from the pedestrian |
| `d_req` | Minimum distance budget for timely pedestrian information and stopping |

The design semester will define speed and latency criteria and the measurement methods for braking distance and clearance. The implementation semester will supply measured values to evaluate this requirement.

## Latency and evaluation plan

The timing plan follows **camera acquisition → pedestrian detection → homography → V2I transmission → fusion/E2E planning → control-command generation**. Each stage records input and output timestamps, giving an overall interval of **`T_system = t6 − t0`**. Planned summaries include **P50, P95, and maximum latency** for use in the distance budget.

| Evaluation area | Planned measurement |
| --- | --- |
| Perception lead | How much earlier, in distance and time, the infrastructure camera detects the pedestrian |
| Information delivery | Stagewise and end-to-end latency from camera acquisition to control-command generation |
| Response lead | Difference in deceleration-onset distance and time between the two systems |
| Safety outcome | Minimum time to collision (TTC) and collision occurrence |

## Vehicle integration and next milestone

The proposed vehicle interfaces are front-camera input, a V2I object-state receiver, and waypoint/target-speed outputs. Safety requirements include **E-stop, command timeout, zero default output, and a watchdog**. These safety functions are planned for implementation and testing.

Next, complete the system requirements and subsystem allocation, define the camera calibration and timestamp conventions, and specify the numerical test conditions. Computing hardware, the MCU, control interfaces, power, and mounting belong to detailed design. A proof of concept and matched driving trials follow those decisions.
