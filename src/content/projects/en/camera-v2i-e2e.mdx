## Research question

**How does adding infrastructure-camera information change an E2E vehicle's response to a pedestrian hidden behind a parked vehicle?**

The team plans a **camera-only versus V2I-assisted E2E comparison** on a small-scale vehicle. A roadside camera would observe the occluded area and send pedestrian information to the vehicle, where it would be combined with the vehicle's camera input and state.

The current outputs are **research summaries, preliminary design, and midterm presentation materials**. The comparison connects these design decisions with subsequent data collection, learning, and vehicle evaluation.

## Literature review and project scope

I reviewed parts of **UniV2X** to understand its vehicle–infrastructure fusion structure and the scope of its open-loop evaluation. I prepared a literature summary to help define a feasible capstone scope and proposed a simplified fusion direction with a later small-scale closed-loop comparison.

This set up the project's question in terms of vehicle behavior: whether earlier pedestrian information changes when the vehicle slows and how much stopping margin it retains.

## Midterm preliminary design

I prepared the midterm presentation materials **after discussions with teammates**. The materials connect the team's problem definition and system concept with pedestrian-risk assessment, braking margins, and training-data requirements.

| Design area | Current proposal |
| --- | --- |
| Representative scenario | One vehicle, one pedestrian, and an occluder on a controlled straight section |
| Operating conditions | Daytime, clear weather, dry pavement, low-speed driving, and normal positioning and V2I reception |
| Infrastructure camera | An elevated oblique view covering several parking spaces, with installation geometry to be assessed on site |
| Pedestrian information | Position, motion, observed risk, and capture time |
| Vehicle planning | Vehicle-camera input and state, with infrastructure information added for the V2I-assisted model |
| Outputs | Waypoints and target speed for a low-level controller |

The camera concept developed from observing a particular gap between parked vehicles toward an elevated view of several parking spaces. Placement, field of view, and pedestrian visibility form part of the planned site evaluation.

The proposed interface separates **infrastructure observations** from **training targets**:

| Information | Proposed use |
| --- | --- |
| Object position, velocity, pedestrian risk, and capture time | Additional input to the V2I-assisted E2E model |
| Vehicle path overlap and braking margin | Inputs to expert rules for generating target-speed labels |
| Expert target speed and trajectory targets | Shared training targets for both models |

The pedestrian-risk feature describes the infrastructure observation. Expert labeling also considers the vehicle's path and stopping margin to specify deceleration, stopping, and restart targets.

## Pedestrian risk and target-speed labels

The preliminary design combines three elements:

- **Pedestrian state:** position relative to the curb and motion toward or away from the road.
- **Path overlap:** whether the pedestrian's occupied area intersects the vehicle's intended path.
- **Braking margin:** distance available relative to system delay, braking distance, and a safety margin.

The stopping-distance budget is expressed as `d_req = v × T_system + D_brake(v) + M`. The team plans to measure the vehicle's braking characteristics and system timing to refine these design quantities.

The expert-label plan uses this context to describe **early deceleration, a held stop while the path is occupied, and restart after the path clears**. The same target labels would be used for both E2E models.

## Infrastructure geometry and time alignment

The preliminary spatial design uses homography to map an image observation onto the ground plane. A common local ENU frame would connect the pedestrian estimate with vehicle position, followed by a conversion into the vehicle's frame.

The timing plan preserves the image's capture timestamp and separates **clock synchronization** from **motion compensation**. Processing, sending, receiving, fusion, and control timestamps would support later analysis of delay and message freshness.

Camera placement, ground reference points, independent accuracy checks, and timestamp conventions are subjects of the current design work.

## Planned E2E comparison

<MediaSlot id="v2i-comparison" />

| Element | Camera-only baseline | V2I-assisted proposal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inputs | Vehicle camera and vehicle state | Vehicle camera, vehicle state, and infrastructure pedestrian information |
| Outputs | Waypoints and target speed | Waypoints and target speed |
| Data source | The same recorded runs | The same recorded runs |
| Training targets | Shared trajectory and expert target-speed labels | The same labels |
| Controlled conditions | Same platform, operating conditions, and low-level controller | The same conditions |

The data plan records vehicle imagery and state together with infrastructure observations during the same run. Training, validation, and test sets would be separated by **run or session**. Planned scenarios include pedestrian absence, standing inside or outside the vehicle path, approach, crossing, and departure.

Evaluation would examine detection and deceleration timing, stopping margin, minimum time to collision, collision occurrence, and unnecessary braking. The goal is to relate any behavioral difference to the added infrastructure information.

## V2I communication research

I am investigating **communication options between the infrastructure and vehicle computers**, including ROS 2/DDS over Wi-Fi. The review focuses on when useful information first reaches the vehicle and how continuously it remains available as the vehicle approaches the scenario.

Current comparison criteria include:

- **Message freshness:** the time from observation to use.
- **Delivery latency:** the time from sending to reception.
- **Reception continuity:** gaps, missed or out-of-order messages, and reconnection behavior.

A dedicated Wi-Fi network and alternative connection arrangements are candidates for team discussion. This work is part of the current **research and design phase**.

## Next design work

The next steps are to refine the communication approach and interface, assess camera visibility and spatial alignment on site, establish timing and braking measurements, and prepare the paired data-collection and evaluation procedure. Model training and the small-scale closed-loop comparison follow this design work.
