## Research question

**Can infrastructure-camera information help an E2E driving system respond earlier to a pedestrian hidden behind a parked vehicle?**

The team plans to compare camera-only driving with V2I-assisted E2E driving on a small-scale vehicle. A roadside camera would observe the occluded area and send pedestrian object state to the vehicle, where it would be combined with vehicle-camera input for planning.

The latest design work focuses on infrastructure-camera placement, pedestrian position, risk zones, coordinate conversion, and time correction. These are foundations for the E2E comparison, which remains the project's final goal.

## Literature review and project scope

I reviewed parts of UniV2X to understand its purpose, vehicle–infrastructure fusion structure, and the limits of open-loop evaluation. I then summarized those points in my own words to help define a feasible scope for our capstone.

From this review, I proposed a simplified fusion approach, with a small-scale closed-loop comparison as a later stage. This would connect the literature review to a concrete question: whether infrastructure information changes the vehicle's response before its camera can see the pedestrian.

## Occlusion scenario and operating conditions

The representative scenario is a pedestrian emerging between parked vehicles. The team selected a camera as the main infrastructure sensor after comparing camera, radar, LiDAR, and smartphone-based approaches. Possible extension to existing CCTV and traffic cameras was one reason for the choice.

| Condition | Initial scope |
| --- | --- |
| Visibility | Daytime, clear weather |
| Road surface | Dry pavement |
| Vehicle motion | Low-speed driving on a straight section |
| Occlusion | A parked vehicle blocks the vehicle camera's view of a pedestrian |

These conditions define the initial **operational design domain (ODD)**: the setting in which the proposed system would be evaluated.

YOLO, ByteTrack, OpenCV, and ROS 2 are being considered for the perception and integration work. The planning design includes a visual encoder, an object-state encoder, and lightweight fusion. These are proposed components for later implementation.

## Infrastructure geometry and time handling

The team is developing the steps needed to turn an infrastructure-camera observation into information the vehicle can use:

- **Position estimation:** use homography to map an image observation to the road plane.
- **Coordinate conversion:** express the road position in the vehicle's coordinate frame.
- **Time correction:** use timestamps to account for the gap between observation and use.
- **Risk zones:** relate the pedestrian's position to areas that call for a warning, deceleration, or a stop.

Camera installation, calibration, position accuracy, and communication timing still need to be resolved through design and later measurement.

## Planned E2E comparison

<MediaSlot id="v2i-comparison" />

| Element | Camera-only baseline | V2I-assisted proposal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inputs | Vehicle camera and vehicle state | Vehicle camera, vehicle state, and infrastructure object state |
| Planning | Visual encoder and E2E planner | Visual and object-state encoders, lightweight fusion, and E2E planner |
| Outputs | Waypoints and target speed | Waypoints and target speed |
| Controlled conditions | Same platform, ODD, speed, training conditions, and low-level controller | Same conditions |

The comparison is intended to isolate the effect of infrastructure information. Planned measures cover how early the pedestrian becomes observable, information delay, deceleration timing, minimum time to collision, and collision occurrence.

The preliminary stopping-distance budget links vehicle speed, system delay, braking distance, and a safety margin. The timing plan separates perception, coordinate conversion, transmission, planning, and command generation so that their contributions can later be measured. These are evaluation plans; no driving or model-performance results are available yet.

## Next design work

The semester's remaining work is to refine subsystem boundaries, object-state fields, communication and timestamp conventions, camera calibration, and the evaluation procedure. Implementation, training, vehicle integration, and closed-loop trials are subsequent stages.
