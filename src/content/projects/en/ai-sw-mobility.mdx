## Engineering question

How can a perceived cone corridor become a trackable path and a reliable stop command when perception and vehicle feedback arrive at different times? My work connects the AEB path and stop logic with constrained steering and integration tests.

## AEB and steering design

| Design choice | Purpose | Implementation evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cone-boundary center path | Turn corridor geometry into a tracking target | AEB path generation and tracking |
| Red-entry stop latch | Preserve the stop decision after the vehicle reaches the gate | Confirmed red-pair handling and latched braking state |
| Steering and steering-rate limits | Bound command magnitude and abrupt changes | Stanley tracking and command limiting |
| Stale-input checks | Request a stop when required path or state data is unavailable | Offline AEB tests and fail-safe branches |

The [AEB implementation and tests](https://github.com/steveandy-sudo/ai-sw-mobility-portfolio) expose behavior at these boundaries. Motion preview was also implemented as part of the path-tracking work. The latest course and stop trials establish successful operation at the reported speeds; isolating the effect of a specific control change requires matched logs.

## Evidence and current result

| Test | Course or reference | Reported result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waypoint driving | Preliminary course | Successful driving at **10 m/s (36 km/h)** |
| Waypoint driving | Final course | Successful driving at **10 m/s (36 km/h)** |
| AEB stop | Competition requirement: **30 km/h** | Successful stops at speeds up to **50 km/h** |

These real-vehicle tests took place on **September 19–20, 2026**, during the organizer's open-practice period at the competition venue. Both courses and AEB were exercised repeatedly. They are practice results, not official preliminary/final competition results.

**AEB success criterion:** detect the red cones, apply braking at the start of the red-cone zone, and come to a complete stop before the end of that zone. Successful stops were reported at speeds up to **50 km/h**, against the **30 km/h** competition requirement.

Waypoint driving and AEB stopping are separate trials; the 50 km/h figure refers to AEB testing. Results were reported in the project update on September 22, 2026.

Earlier development included Gazebo closed-loop validation, initial real-vehicle trials at approximately 7 km/h, investigation of steering oscillation, and a steering-motor delay correction by the control team. The repository also contains 29 AEB tests checked during source collection. These development records are retained separately from the latest course results.

## Follow-up measurements

The next analysis will quantify the successful course and stop trials using synchronized speed, steering, path, and brake records. A matched earlier recording is needed to isolate the effect of the steering correction.

| Record | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Time-aligned target and measured steering | Does the actuator follow the requested angle, and with what delay? |
| Target path and vehicle trajectory | How large are lateral tracking errors along the route? |
| Braking request, measured speed, and stop position | Does the vehicle stop at the intended location? |
| Trial count and individual outcomes | Does the behavior repeat under the same conditions? |

Trial counts, stopping distances, and path-error statistics will be added with the corresponding records. No success rate or quantified reduction in steering oscillation is inferred from the reported successful runs.
