## AEB and steering design

| Design choice | Purpose | Implementation evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cone-boundary center path | Turn corridor geometry into a tracking target | AEB path generation and tracking |
| Red-entry stop latch | Preserve the stop decision after the vehicle reaches the gate | Confirmed red-pair handling and latched braking state |
| Steering and steering-rate limits | Bound command magnitude and abrupt changes | Pure Pursuit and Stanley tracking, and command limiting |
| Stale-input checks | Request a stop when required path or state data is unavailable | Offline AEB tests and fail-safe branches |

The AEB logic checks input freshness and preserves the stop state after the red-cone trigger. The path-tracking implementation combines Pure Pursuit and Stanley and also includes motion preview.

## High-level control and technical report

I authored **Section 3.4, High-Level Control**, of Team K.A.I.'s competition technical report, dated **September 24, 2026** (pp. 186–191). The section explains how a target path, target speed, and stop request become steering, speed, and brake commands for the vehicle.

The controller combines Pure Pursuit and Stanley steering at **50 Hz**. Their weights change with straight driving, corner approach, turning, and corner exit. Lookahead distance adapts to speed and upcoming curvature; gradual weight changes and steering-rate limits control command transitions. The interface sends target speed, steering angle, a brake request, and a timestamp to the low-level controller.

## Matched simulation comparison

I conducted the comparison tests and analyzed the logs for **hybrid Pure Pursuit + Stanley versus Stanley alone**. The comparison used **the same Gazebo course segments at 6 m/s**, with route progress aligned to reduce differences in segment composition and travel distance.

| Metric | Change with hybrid tracking versus Stanley alone |
| --- | --- |
| Heading-error p95 | Approximately **14% lower** |
| Steering-error p95 | Approximately **7% lower** |
| Steering-command total variation | Approximately **10% lower** |
| Curved-section lateral-error p95 | Approximately **8% lower** |

Here, **p95** is the 95th percentile of the recorded error values. Steering-command total variation measures the accumulated changes in the steering command over the comparison segment. These results are recorded in **Section 4.3.1 (pp. 239–240)** and the **performance summary on p. 256** of the team technical report.

## Real-vehicle trials

| Test | Course or reference | Reported result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waypoint driving | Preliminary course | Successful driving at **10 m/s (36 km/h)** |
| Waypoint driving | Final course | Successful driving at **10 m/s (36 km/h)** |
| AEB command trial | Trial speed: **50 km/h**; competition requirement: **30 km/h** | Stop command issued at the red cones; vehicle overran the stopping zone due to a reported brake hardware fault |

These real-vehicle trials took place on **September 19–20, 2026**, during the organizer's open-practice period at the competition venue. They included repeated waypoint runs on both courses and separate AEB trials.

**AEB success criterion:** detect the red cones, apply braking at the zone entrance, and stop before the zone ends. In the **50 km/h** trial, the stop command was issued at the red cones. With **two of the four brakes reported inoperative**, the vehicle passed the stopping-zone end.

Earlier development included Gazebo closed-loop testing, initial real-vehicle trials at approximately 7 km/h, investigation of steering oscillation, and a steering-motor delay correction by the control team.

## Follow-up measurements

The simulation comparison provides a baseline for the next vehicle tests. Further work will examine repeatability across vehicle runs and measure the delay between an AEB command and vehicle deceleration using synchronized speed, steering, path, and brake records.

| Record | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Time-aligned target and measured steering | Does the actuator follow the requested angle, and with what delay? |
| Target path and vehicle trajectory across repeated runs | How do vehicle tracking errors vary by course segment and speed? |
| Braking request, measured speed, and stop position | Does the vehicle stop at the intended location? |
| Trial count and individual outcomes | Does the behavior repeat under the same conditions? |

Per-trial counts and stopping distances remain to be documented for the venue-practice runs.
