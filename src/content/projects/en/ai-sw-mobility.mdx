## Driving task

The vehicle follows a recorded route, handles traffic lights and stop events, avoids obstacles, and returns to its reference path. My work connects behavior planning with the vehicle: defining FSM states with teammates, implementing hybrid tracking, examining recorded driving data, and operating field tests.

## FSM-based behavior planning

I worked with teammates to define the **states and transition conditions** used by the ROS 2 behavior planner. The design connects the current mission and perception inputs with a target path, target speed, and stop request.

| Driving task | Behavior managed by the decision software |
| --- | --- |
| Route following | Follow the route and apply the active course and speed constraints |
| Traffic-light handling | Approach, decide whether to pass or brake, wait, and restart |
| Timed stops | Approach, stop, hold for the required period, and release |
| Obstacle avoidance | Reduce speed, follow an avoidance path, and return to the reference route |
| Mission completion | Approach the finish and complete the stopping sequence |

The team implementation uses a shared stop-point state machine for signal, timed-stop, and finish events. Obstacle behavior manages its path and speed requests alongside these mission events. An arbiter combines the module outputs before motion control produces vehicle commands.

## GNSS waypoint collection and driving

I recorded **GNSS coordinates while driving the course** to establish route waypoints, then conducted vehicle trials using GNSS/IMU-based localization. This connected route preparation with waypoint following on the vehicle.

During venue practice, waypoint-following runs on **both the preliminary and final courses reached up to 11 m/s (39.6 km/h)**.

## Recorded-drive analysis and debugging

I used **Foxglove and ROS 2 rosbag2/MCAP recordings** to investigate driving behavior. I traced perception outputs, behavior-state transitions, planned paths, and vehicle feedback together, checking their timestamps and coordinate frames.

The review followed the full sequence:

1. Locate the observed behavior in the recorded run.
2. Follow the relevant perception, FSM, path, and vehicle-state topics.
3. Review the evidence and discuss corrective actions with teammates.
4. Return to field testing and inspect the resulting vehicle behavior.

My main contribution to obstacle-avoidance work combined **log analysis, proposals for corrections, and field operation**. Recorded data helped narrow down issues and made debugging more systematic and efficient.

## From presentation to implementation

### Presenting the control strategy

I presented a **Pure Pursuit–Stanley hybrid tracking strategy** at a Team K.A.I. technical symposium sponsored by Hyundai Mobis. The presentation, prepared with Woojin Hwang, compared Pure Pursuit's lookahead-based steering with Stanley's heading- and cross-track-error feedback.

The proposal combined their steering outputs, using driving conditions to guide the balance between them. It also considered gradual changes in the controller weights and a ROS 2 simulation comparison.

### Applying the strategy in ROS 2

After the presentation, I wrote and revised code for the combined controller. The implementation calculates both steering commands and blends them by driving phase: straight driving, corner approach, turning, and corner exit. Weight changes are limited from one control step to the next; speed and upcoming path geometry also influence lookahead and the individual steering calculations.

This work connected the strategy discussed in the presentation with **controller implementation, comparison tests, and log analysis**.

### Recognition

The presentation by **Woojin Hwang and Junghun Hwang** received the **Hyundai Mobis Technical Special Award, Autonomous Driving Division**, awarded in the name of **Konkuk University Team K.A.I.**

## Initial simulation results

I compared hybrid tracking with Stanley alone on **matched Gazebo course segments at 6 m/s** and analyzed the recorded results.

| Metric | Initial hybrid-tracking result versus Stanley alone |
| --- | --- |
| Heading-error p95 | Approximately **14% lower** |
| Steering-command total variation | Approximately **10% lower** |

Here, **p95** is the 95th percentile of the recorded error values. Steering-command total variation measures accumulated changes in the steering command over the comparison segment. These figures describe the initial matched simulation comparison.

## Real-vehicle trials

### Obstacle avoidance and route return

I operated the vehicle during obstacle-avoidance field tests and checked the complete behavior sequence: **deceleration before avoidance, collision-free passage, return to the reference route, and acceleration afterward**. Each part of this sequence was observed to work in the field tests.

Foxglove analysis and discussions with teammates connected observations from these runs with the next round of software adjustments.

### Venue-practice waypoint and AEB trials

| Test | Course or reference | Observed behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waypoint driving | Preliminary course | Successful driving at speeds up to **11 m/s (39.6 km/h)** |
| Waypoint driving | Final course | Successful driving at speeds up to **11 m/s (39.6 km/h)** |
| AEB command trial | Approach speed: **50 km/h** | Red-cone detection triggered the stop request at the stopping-zone entrance |

The waypoint and AEB trials took place during the organizer's open-practice period at the competition venue. The AEB footage documents **red-cone recognition and stop-command triggering**.

The cone-course AEB workflow forms a path from perceived cone boundaries and latches the red-cone stop request. I used Gazebo, Foxglove, and vehicle tests to inspect the generated path, steering command, and stop state.

## What I learned

The project made **recorded driving data** central to my debugging process. Tracing the inputs, decision states, and measured vehicle response together helped me narrow down problems and discuss concrete corrections with the team.

Working through both software review and field operation also connected FSM transitions with the behavior I checked on the vehicle: when it slows, how it passes an obstacle, and how it resumes route driving.
