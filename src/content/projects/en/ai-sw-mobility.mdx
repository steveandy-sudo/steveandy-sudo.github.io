### Offline comparison and temporary horizon setting

A condition introduced on or after October 21 excluded the second obstacle row from collision checks if the **35 m** local path could not contain both that row and the return target. The recorded geometry required about **40.4–41.3 m** to include the end of the second row and the **12 m** return target.

The offline comparison replayed the same three October 17 K-City logs through the planner, removing only the obstacle exclusion condition. At 10 ms evaluation intervals, the first path accounting for both rows appeared **1.10–1.23 s earlier**. [Offline comparison record](https://github.com/steveandy-sudo/ai-sw-mobility-portfolio/blob/main/docs/EXPERIMENTS.md#offline-comparison-and-temporary-horizon-setting)

I separately committed a temporary **35 to 45 m** horizon setting (`2eb64af`) and kept the return target at **12 m**. In offline recalculation with the 45 m setting, some path candidates still geometrically overlapped observed obstacles. Both personal changes are included in the team `main` and `kgm` branches.

## Driving task

The vehicle follows a recorded route, handles traffic lights and stop events, and avoids obstacles before returning to its reference path.

## FSM-based behavior planning

I defined states and transition conditions for the ROS 2 behavior planner. The planner uses the current mission and perception inputs to select a target path, target speed, and stop request.

| Driving task | Behavior managed by the decision software |
| --- | --- |
| Route following | Follow the route and apply the active course and speed constraints |
| Traffic-light handling | Approach, decide whether to pass or brake, wait, and restart |
| Timed stops | Approach, stop, hold for the required period, and release |
| Obstacle avoidance | Reduce speed, follow an avoidance path, and return to the reference route |
| Mission completion | Approach the finish and complete the stopping sequence |

The software uses a shared stop-point state machine for signal, timed-stop, and finish events. Obstacle behavior manages its path and speed requests alongside these mission events. An arbiter combines the module outputs before motion control produces vehicle commands.

## GNSS waypoint collection and driving

I recorded GNSS coordinates while driving the course to establish route waypoints, then conducted vehicle trials using GNSS/IMU-based localization.

During venue practice, waypoint-following runs on **both the preliminary and final courses reached up to 11 m/s (39.6 km/h)**.

## From presentation to implementation

### Presenting the control strategy

I presented a **Pure Pursuit–Stanley hybrid tracking strategy** at a Team K.A.I. technical symposium sponsored by Hyundai Mobis. The presentation compared Pure Pursuit's lookahead-based steering with Stanley's heading- and cross-track-error feedback.

The proposal combined their steering outputs, using driving conditions to guide the balance between them. It also considered gradual changes in the controller weights and a ROS 2 simulation comparison.

### Applying the strategy in ROS 2

After the presentation, I implemented and revised a ROS 2 controller that blends Pure Pursuit and Stanley steering commands by driving phase: straight driving, corner approach, turning, and corner exit. The controller limits weight changes between control steps and adjusts lookahead and steering calculations using vehicle speed and upcoming path geometry.

I wrote the high-level control section of the competition vehicle's technical report, describing the path-tracking and command-generation pipeline.

### Recognition

The presentation by **Woojin Hwang and Junghun Hwang** received the **Hyundai Mobis Technical Special Award, Autonomous Driving Division**, awarded in the name of **Konkuk University Team K.A.I.**
