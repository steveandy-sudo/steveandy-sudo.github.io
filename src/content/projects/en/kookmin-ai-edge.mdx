## Competition context

The project brought together perception, driving decisions, control integration, and parking on a real competition vehicle. As team lead in a five-member team, I worked on the learning pipeline, object-detection workflow, mission integration, and vehicle tuning.

Preliminary and final rankings describe the **team's competition performance**.

My work crossed three parts of the team system:

| Workstream | My implementation or integration | System context |

| Vehicle and mission integration | Mission Manager, Final Driver, mission-state handling, and parameter tuning | Integration into the shared competition stack |

## Learning pipeline

The ROS 2 imitation-learning workflow covered driving-data collection, model training, TorchScript export, and real-time inference for XYCAR. I implemented hybrid logic to switch between learning-based driving and deterministic traffic-light and mission handling.

Learning-based driving was explored and implemented, but steering oscillation remained. The final competition driving strategy used rule-based driving; the existence of the learning pipeline should not be read as evidence that it was the final deployed driving method.

## YOLO & Roboflow

I managed the dataset workflow from upload through annotation, annotation review, class organization, augmentation, train/validation/test splits, and export. I directly trained one unified YOLO object-detection model for traffic lights, vehicles, obstacles, and cones.

## Lessons & scope

This project connected model development with mission logic and the practical constraints of a shared vehicle. The main engineering lesson is visible in the deployment record: an implemented approach may still need to be set aside when its behavior remains unresolved.

The linked project repository records the source versions of the selected packages. This case study distinguishes development work, the final driving strategy, and team competition outcomes.
